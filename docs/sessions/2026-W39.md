# Week 39, 21 to 27 September 2026

One file per week, to match the weekly board in Notion. Short entries, written the day they happen.
The detail (numbers, methods, dead ends) belongs in the topic docs and in `reports/`; this file is
what happened and when.

## Entries

### 2026-09-21 (Monday)

- Lead meeting produced three questions about multi-class and multi-label data: how to prepare
  such data, whether YOLO26 can be used for it at all (her view: probably not, with a multi-level
  CNN as the fallback), and whether YOLO can take two box labels on one picture at training time.
  She asked for a prepared pipeline tested on what we have, since new images are delayed.
- Answered all three from evidence rather than opinion. Measured the label space first: two tiers,
  8,409 pictures, 6,243 with a frame-level tag, 2,179 with an object label, 13 with both, and
  **55 pictures with more than one label (0.65 per cent)**, split 42 two-box and 13 tag-and-box.
- Findings that change the shape of the question: **47 of the 434 annotated pictures carry a second
  label the box file does not carry**, because the annotation ran 10 tasks, one per box class, so
  co-occurring objects of another class were never marked. **33 of the 55 conflicts were read as one
  naming conflict** (Extraneous Polymer against Fiber), and the single-label rule trains 34 of the 36
  Extraneous Polymer pictures as Fiber. **No picture carries two frame-level tags.** The box tier
  covers 434 pictures against 6,243 for the tag tier, and Folded Lens and Stretched Bubble have no
  pictures at all.
- **Later the same day the naming-conflict reading was tested on the frames and corrected.** The
  object was cropped out of all 33 contested pictures on the VM and judged blind against two
  reference groups from the same folders. Of 17 objects both folders call Extraneous Polymer, 14 are
  a thick mass of varying width. Of 17 both call Fiber, 13 are a thin filament. Of the 34 contested
  objects, 29 are thin filaments. So the two classes are not two words for one thing, the older
  library's Fiber name matches the frames, and the client question changes from "which vocabulary
  wins" to "were these pictures misfiled in the newer folder". The same risk reaches 217 of the 267
  pictures the newer folder calls Extraneous Polymer, which have no copy in the older library and so
  have never been checked by a second source. Evidence: `run/ep_fiber_check/20260921/`.
- Found on the VM while doing that check, undocumented in `HANDOFF.md`: a 47 GB
  `20260826_T24_Production_Sample` sitting beside the two updated folders, with a `sample folder`
  and a `sample metadata.csv`, plus `Package Find Test`, `Ripple Obstruction`, `Utils` and
  `YOLO26_ICube_Defects`. The dataset tree on the VM totals 105 GB. This is relevant to "new images
  are delayed" and needs a look before it is quoted anywhere.
- **The asked-for test was built and run: the multi-label pictures alone cannot carry a detector.**
  47 of the 55 carry boxes (the other 8 are listed with their reason: 4 HEMA pairs, 2 Multiple Lenses
  against Wet Package, 2 Low Dose against Wet Package). The split is 70/30 by picture rather than
  80/20, because at this size the training side is flat while the measurement is fragile, so the
  pictures went to the test side: 34 train against 13 test, 85 boxes against 32, seed 42. Two tests,
  one variable (the split), usual settings (YOLO26n, 100 epochs, imgsz 1280, batch 8, patience 50).
  Test 1 scored mAP50 0.4741 with a precision of 0.3383 and a recall of 0.6555. Test 2, the same
  pictures with the whole of line 25 held out, scored 0.0758 with a precision of 0.0103: a factor of
  6.2, so the number from the plain split is memorisation of one capture session. Test 2 stopped
  itself at 87 epochs. Both tests ran twice with identical numbers. Evidence:
  `run/multilabel_test_20260921/`.
- Measured while doing it, and it reframes the whole question: **0 of the 434 pictures in the
  existing detection set carry two classes in the box file** (0 of 346 train, 0 of 88 test). Every
  detection number this project holds, including the 0.5355 baseline, was measured on pictures with
  one class. And each of the 47 multi-label pictures also has exactly one class in its box file, so
  their multi-label nature lives in the folder record alone and the two-class case cannot be
  exercised on what we hold.
- Checked the tool at the pinned build instead of trusting memory: the detection label reader takes
  one row per object with a class per row and has no one-class-per-picture rule, so the lead's
  question 3 is a yes; the classification reader is folder-per-class with a single-answer loss, so
  multi-label classification is not available in 8.4.142, and the upstream request is still open
  after more than two years.
- Consequence recorded as the main recommendation: the label **tier**, not the label **count**,
  decides the model. Tags go to a classifier (and our own classifier can take a sigmoid head with
  binary cross-entropy, which the tool cannot), boxes go to the detector, and the near-term path is
  the cascade, not a new architecture. The lead's fallback, "multi-level CNN", is already built
  twice in this project, so it is a head change rather than a new direction.
- New measurement scripts and evidence: `experiments/multilabel/two_tier_eda.py` and
  `verify_two_tier_eda.py` (102 checks, 0 failed), output in `run/multilabel_eda/20260921/`. The
  box counts independently match the client's own ontology table on all 10 labels.
- Documents written: the strategy report with the options, costs, interpretation rules, conflicts,
  pitfalls and debt; the folder protocol; and a self-contained message for the lead. The message
  has not been sent.
- Registry grew from 48 to 54 rows, all six new ones ideated: the multi-hot tag manifest, the
  two-class-picture proof run, the sigmoid tag head, the cascade, the debris-family merge, and the
  missing-box re-annotation.
