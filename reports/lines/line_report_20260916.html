Can one model handle every production line?

This page counts what we have, line by line, and sets out the plan and the open decisions. The three folders hold 12,079 images across 5 production lines. After removing repeated pictures they hold 10,614 unique pictures, of which 8,401 carry one of the 22 defect classes and every one carries a line number, so no image is unassigned. Because the folders label the same classes, the analysis pools them by class rather than by folder, and the pooled set is what the models use. To answer the client question we train 5 separate ResNet-50 models, one per line, and compare them on the classes that three or more lines share (17 classes). If those models agree on the classes they have in common, the line carries no signal and one model is enough.

So those 7 classes bring 175 new images, not 313.

The overlap was checked twice, by file name and by content, and the two agree in one direction only. Of the older library's 1,465 files, 1,249 are found in the new drop by name and 1,287 by content, so a name comparison misses 38 files that are the same picture under a different name. In the other direction the names are trustworthy: no file name appears in two folders over different content, so a name match is never a false match. Taken across all three folders, 187 pictures are filed under more than one name, 122 of them across folders and 65 of them inside a single folder.

The three pool rules in section 3 resolve these conflicts, and the arithmetic closes. Of the 418 pictures that carry two labels, 86 are the Low Dose pair and are therefore one class, 271 involve "Clear" or "duplicates", which are not classes, and 10 pair HEMA with a defect class, which leaves one class standing. That leaves 51 pictures that keep two defect classes, and the older library decides those. This is how the pooled set in section 3 counts every picture once and still gives every picture a single label.

What the comparison between the five models requires

The five training sets would be unequal: L24 would train on 1,995 pictures and L25 on 664, three times fewer, so a gap between those two models could come from the training volume rather than from the line. Equalising the volume is practical: train every line on 664 pictures, the count of the smallest line, drawn in proportion to that line's own class mix. Equalising per class instead, the same number of pictures for every class on every line, is not practical: on its scarcest line a class can hold a single picture (HEMA Obstruction 1, Bubble Cluster 1, Bubble Irregular 1, Bubble Scatter 1), which would cut every line to about 181 training pictures against the 1,995 to 664 available today.

The five models carry different class lists, so their overall accuracy is not directly comparable: a model carrying 16 classes has a different chance level from one carrying 21. The comparable quantity is the per-class recall on the classes the lines share, which is what the comparison column in section 3 counts.

The thin cells bound what can be concluded. 35 class-line cells hold fewer than five test images, and the weakest lines are L27 and L31, where 7 and 9 classes respectively produce fewer than five test images. A stated tolerance, or a confidence interval per recall, is needed before any difference there can be called real or not.

A direct test of the premise

The case for one model rests on the capture method being identical, so that a picture carries no trace of its line. All 12,079 files are the same format, 2,048 by 2,448 greyscale BMP of 5,014,582 bytes, so there is no resolution difference to find. Whether anything else in the pixels marks the line is testable in one short run: train a classifier to predict the line from the pooled pictures. If it cannot beat chance, the premise holds and the single-model conclusion rests on solid ground.

6 · Questions to settle

Training is on hold until these are answered. The first group we can decide. The second group needs a client or lead answer.

For us to decide

  • Confirm the three rules that build the pool. They are: "Low Dose" and "Low Dose Obstructing Region of Interest" are one class; "Clear", "HEMA" and "duplicates" are not classes, so 2,213 pictures fall outside the 22-class set; and the 51 pictures carrying two defect classes take the class the older library assigns. Every number in this page depends on those three.
  • Volume-matched training sets, or the full line data? L24 would train on 1,995 pictures and L25 on 664. Matching the volume, by training every line on 664 pictures drawn in proportion to its own class mix, makes a gap attributable to the line rather than to the volume. Matching per class instead would cut every line to about 181 pictures, because on its scarcest line a class can hold a single picture.
  • What counts as similar? An explicit tolerance for the recall comparison, for example within five points, or non-overlapping confidence intervals. Without a number, the result cannot be called the same or different.
  • Keep the comparable cut at three or more lines? That is 17 of the 22 classes, giving between three and ten line pairs each. Another 3 classes are scorable on exactly two lines, so widening the cut to two lines would add 3 classes, and each of them yields a single line pair.
  • Do we run the line-predictability check? One short run: if a classifier cannot tell which line a picture came from, the premise behind a single model is confirmed at the pixel level rather than inferred from five results.

For the client or the lead

  • Do the 7 classes that only the older library carries still count? They are Bubble, Bubble Cluster, Bubble Irregular, Bubble On 123, Bubble On Edge, HEMA Fragment, Wet Package, holding 313 pictures of which 175 exist nowhere else. Pooling the folders does not answer this, because no other folder labels them.
  • If "Clear" is later ruled to be a class, it returns as a 23rd class. 2,213 pictures carry Clear as their only label, and they are excluded from the 22-class pool today. If they come back, every per-line number in this page changes.
  • Is a test set of 287 to 858 images per line enough, given the thin cells? The pool gives each line 287 to 858 test images, but 35 class-line cells still hold fewer than five, and on L27 and L31 that is 7 and 9 classes. Those results will carry wide error bars.
  • Four classes cannot support the line-by-line comparison. Bubble On 123, Dirty Camera are scorable on one line only, and Bubble Scatter, Dirty Strobe, HEMA Fragment on two lines, so their results stand alone. Confirm that is acceptable.

7 · Recommendations

In order. Each one states its evidence in the same sentence.

  1. Run the line-predictability check before the five models. It costs one short run, and if a classifier cannot tell which line a picture came from, the premise behind one model is confirmed at the pixel level rather than inferred from five results.
  2. Use the pooled class-centric dataset, not a single folder. Pooling the three folders by defect class gives 8,401 pictures across 22 classes, which raises the classes scorable on all five lines from 4 to 13 and the test images per line from 34 to 239 up to 287 to 858.
  3. Record the three pool rules and keep them fixed. Every number in this page depends on the rename of Low Dose, on Clear, HEMA and duplicates not being classes (2,213 pictures excluded), and on the older library deciding the class for the 51 pictures that carry two.
  4. Split each line at 70:30. It leaves a test image for 87 class-line cells against 78 at 80:20, and it scores the models on 2,525 test images against 1,676. The 849 pictures that 80:20 buys back are not worth the 9 cells it costs.
  5. Match the training volume across the five models, not the per-class counts. L24 would train on 1,995 pictures and L25 on 664, so an unmatched comparison would confound the line with the training volume and could be attributed to neither. Train every line on 664 pictures drawn in proportion to its own class mix; matching per class would cut every line to about 181 pictures, because on its scarcest line a class can hold a single picture.
  6. Compare per-class recall on the shared classes, not overall accuracy. The five models carry different class lists, so a model with 16 classes has a different chance level from one with 21. The comparison set is the 17 classes that three or more lines can score.
  7. State the tolerance before the run. 35 class-line cells hold fewer than five test images, so only a declared tolerance or a confidence interval can turn the result into a yes or no.
  8. Report the classes that cannot be compared separately. Bubble On 123, Dirty Camera are scorable on one line and Bubble Scatter, Dirty Strobe, HEMA Fragment on two, so their numbers stand alone and must not be read as a cross-line result.
