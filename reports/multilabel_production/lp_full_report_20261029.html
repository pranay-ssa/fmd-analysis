Can one model report every lens class at once?

A single image model was asked to report every LP class visible in a lens picture, including the case where nothing is wrong. 1,344 pictures were held back and never seen during training. Every figure here is read from the measured results of the two runs, and the same numbers appear in the companion workbook.

Average precision asks how well the model ranks pictures: line the pictures up by how sure the model is, and are the true ones near the top. It runs from 0 to 1, where 1 is perfect and 0.5 is a coin toss. Accuracy is not used, because most pictures are normal and a model that always says "nothing wrong" would look excellent and be useless.

5. Why each LP class scored as it did

Only the classes with at least twenty test pictures are explained. The four rare ones are shown above but cannot support a conclusion either way.

  • Missing Primary Package. The most consistent LP class in the set and the largest test group. The model ranks it almost perfectly and still finds 99.3 percent of the pictures at the default cut-off.
  • No Lens. An empty cavity looks different from every other case. Ranking is almost perfect and recall at the default cut-off is 0.91.
  • Multiple Lens. A large, visually distinct class. Strong ranking and a balanced precision and recall, so the number is trustworthy.
  • Low Dose. Now well above its 0.70 target at 0.92, on three times the test support, with recall 0.76 at the default cut-off. The unfinished-annotation caveat from the previous run carries less weight now that the class has 135 test pictures, but a marking pass on the older files would still settle it.
  • Cavity Off Center. Only 37 test pictures. The shift of the cavity is a strong visual cue; the small dip against the previous run is within what a group this size can wobble.
  • Lens Off Center. Still below its 0.90 target, at 0.82. The lens position also changes with multiple lens and cavity offset, so the three overlap; the best balance is 0.78 at a lower cut-off. Twice the test support moved it from 0.75 to 0.82.
  • Dirty Camera. Below its 0.85 target at 0.72, but much improved: nearly doubling the test group lifted the score from 0.58 and recall at the default cut-off from 0.03 to 0.39. The best balance, 0.69, comes at a 0.30 cut-off. What remains looks like the intrinsic difficulty of diffuse, low-contrast dirt rather than a data shortage.
  • Folded Lens. Better, and still the weakest class with enough pictures to judge: 0.49 against its 0.70 target, up from 0.27 on twice the test support. Everything the model flags is right, but at the default cut-off it finds only 4.5 percent of them; the best balance is 0.51 at a 0.20 cut-off. Almost every folded lens in the data also carries multiple lens, so the overlap remains a labelling finding, not only a training one.

6. The thirteenth class: one OOI flag

Run 2 adds a single answer that covers all eight OOI classes together. It is not a new finding and it does not say which OOI class is present; it says that at least one is, which is what the routing step needs to decide whether a picture goes on to the object stage.

Because it routes rather than describes, it is judged by how often it fires, not by average precision.

This is the behaviour the routing step needs, and on this annotation it meets both of its targets outright: it fires on 99.3 percent of object-only pictures against a 90 percent target, and stops on 99.5 percent of clean pictures against a 5 percent limit. The conditional pass and fail block can be built on this.

7. What this tells us

Four findings, each carrying the number that supports it.

  • The average clears the 0.85 bar for the first time. 0.851 against the pre-registered 0.85, up from 0.783, with Low Dose 0.92 and Dirty Camera 0.72 on much larger test groups.
  • Three LP classes still hold the ceiling away, for three different reasons. Folded Lens 0.49 is a label overlap problem, Dirty Camera 0.72 is intrinsically hard, and Lens Off Center 0.82 overlaps with two other classes. Each improved as its support grew, which points at data, not the model, as the main lever.
  • Folded Lens is better but not solved. Twice the support nearly doubled the score, still short of its 0.70 target. Almost every folded lens also carries multiple lens; marking folded lenses that appear alone, or reporting the pair as one class, is the next lever.
  • The routing block is ready. The object flag meets both of its targets (99.3 percent on object-only pictures, 0.5 percent on Clear), so the conditional pass and fail layer can be designed on measured behaviour.

8. What we would do next

In order, each tied to a finding above.

  1. Design the conditional pass and fail block on the routing behaviour just measured: Clear passes, bubbles pass at an acceptable size, gated classes fail, and the remaining presentation classes need a ruling before they enter the block.
  2. Report Folded Lens at a lower cut-off, or together with Multiple Lens, and state which reading is meant whenever the number is quoted.
  3. Decide the open threshold questions with the client: the micron cut for the four gated classes, the size bound that makes a bubble acceptable, and pass or fail for the six presentation classes the specification does not rule on.
