The adopted scale of 15.337 microns per pixel is derived from the documented 300 micron cut. The customer's micron-threshold tags switch at a defect longer side of 19.41 to 19.61 px, and 300 divided by the 19.51 px midpoint gives the scale, matching the frozen central figure of 15.3369 with an interval of 15.298 to 15.376. The old 647 micron cut inverts to the same scale, from a boundary at 42.08 to 42.79 px, and is the independent reproduction.

Limitation, stated plainly and not resolved here. The lens nominal diameter is not documented in anything received. The only documented ruler is the customer's own 300 micron cut. A written lens diameter from the customer would close the remaining question.

5. Does the scale differ by folder group or by production line

The lens is the same physical part on every line, so a real difference in measured rim pixels between strata would be a real scale difference. The absolute test is blocked for lack of a blind nominal.

No production line and no folder group differs from another beyond the measurement noise. Between strata the raw rim median runs 688 to 763 px, a range of 75 px, 10.2 percent of the mean. Within a stratum the relative spread runs 10.0 to 25.7 percent. The within-stratum spread dwarfs the differences among the candidate scales (15.337, 18.1165 and 18.25), so measurement alone cannot settle a scale question of that size. On the clean band the strata agree to within 6.8 percent.

The folder disagreement, and why two figures differ

The widely quoted 113 figure is a NAME-based count, not a content count. On content identity the overlap is larger, not smaller: 279 pictures library-wide and 129 on the production batch, because a name route cannot see a picture that was renamed. 113 is the name-based batch figure. The three library folders label these pictures differently from each other, and the content figures are the ones the priority rule acts on.

10. What to do with it

Ordered actions, each carrying the number that makes it necessary.

  1. Ask the customer for a written lens diameter. The adopted scale of 15.337 microns per pixel rests on the customer's own documented 300 micron cut, and it is the only documented ruler received. The lens nominal is undocumented, so a written lens diameter from the customer would close the remaining scale question.
  2. Ask which reading of a box's size the threshold script uses. The data supports the longer side of the box (0 misfiled images) over the diagonal (39 misfiled on foreign matter, 4 on fiber), and the declared cut moved by exactly the ratio the client stated, but the proxy itself is our inference, not a written rule.
  3. Settle the two code collisions. The shared HEMA code and the two view-obstructed prefixes decide whether two pairs of defects merge, which moves every count that carries them across the 28 canonical defects.
  4. Decide the multi-label training scheme. A single picture can carry up to 11 codes, and the median picture carries 1, so a one-folder-per-class split discards most of what the annotators wrote.
  5. Decide whether the unannotated pictures are wanted as explicit negatives. Coverage of the production blob folder is 10.03%, so the remainder is available at no labelling cost if a run needs them.
  6. Treat the region-annotation classes as regions, not detector targets. Their boxes span more than the whole lens, so a detector target read would be wrong at that scale.
