# Scale candidates, error-bounded intervals, and the four falsification tests

Phases 2, 3 and 4 of the scale plan.

Phase 0 established where the micron side came from: one candidate nominal, our
own 14,200 micron lens diameter, and it is an unsourced assumption. Phase 1
settled the pixel side: the lens rim is the dark ring at 0.89 of the cavity
radius, the lens diameter is 789.0 px median (IQR 764.4 to 809.5, relative spread
5.71 percent) over 2,659 pictures, with a method range of 760.0 to 789.0 px and a
population split of 777.3 px on the library against 809.5 to 811.6 px on the
production drop.

This record finishes the job. Phase 2 enumerates every candidate external length
and labels each one documented or assumed, with its source. Phase 3 combines the
pixel measurement with each candidate into a scale INTERVAL with an error budget,
never a bare point, reads the client gate back into microns at each candidate
scale, and states the tension between the two readings without resolving it.
Phase 4 runs four falsification tests.

Nothing here was resolved by preference. The tension is quantified, the two
readings it allows are named, and the evidence that would separate them is given.

## Phase 2, every candidate external length

Five candidates were enumerated. Two are documented in a received document, one
is our own working assumption, and two do not exist anywhere. The rule enforced
in code: no assumed nominal is ever presented as documented.

| Candidate | Value | Class | Where it is written |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominal lens diameter | 14,200 microns | ASSUMED, our own figure | `/data/FMD_Data_26082026/measure_scale.py` line 9 `NOMINAL_LENS_DIAMETER_UM = 14_200.0`; `eda_size_um.py` line 24; the only rationale is `eda_methodology.html` section 21.3 line 393, an uncited "soft daily contact lens; spec range 14.0 to 14.3 mm" |
| Client 300 micron cut | 300 microns | DOCUMENTED | `docs/FM3 Edge Algorithm URS Final Draft .pdf` section 4.2, size bands under 300, 300 to 500, 500 to 647 microns; annotator handover email 2026-10-13 |
| Client 647 micron threshold | 647 microns | DOCUMENTED | same URS, section 1.1.1 p3, Requirement 1 p7, section 6.2 p28 |
| Package, blister, cavity or tray dimension | none | NOT FOUND | searched, no received document and no JnJ line spec carries one |
| Camera or optics object-space scale (pixel pitch, working distance, magnification) | none | NOT FOUND | searched, explicitly absent per Phase 0 and `eda_methodology.html` section 21.1 |

The first three are the candidates that can act as a ruler. The fourth and fifth
were searched for and found absent, and are recorded as absent rather than
skipped.

### Why 14,200 is an assumption and not a documented nominal

Phase 0 is explicit and this phase verified it rather than repeating it: 14,200
is the hardcoded constant every scale figure divides into. Its one stated
rationale, in our own earlier artifact, is that soft daily contact lenses have a
spec range of 14.0 to 14.3 mm. No received document states that range, and
`docs/reference/received_docs_nominals.md` says the lens diameter "is written in
no received document present here" and that 14,200 "is our own figure". So the
value is an assumption about the product, and it is carried as an assumption
here. It is not a manufacturer figure and not a measurement.

### Why 300 and 647 are documented

Both are in the received URS. 647 microns is the foreign-matter threshold
(Requirement 1: detection of objects 647 um or larger). The 300 micron cut is the
first nice-to-have band edge, and the annotator handover email of 2026-10-13
states the 300 micron cut. The gate tags in the production files switch on these
cuts, and the switch is reachable directly: the flagged versus unflagged boundary
on the 2026-10-15 files sits at 19.41 to 19.61 px. Inverting it gives a scale
that does not depend on our lens assumption at all, which is exactly why it is a
candidate.

## Phase 3, the scale as an interval, with its error budget

### The lens-implied scale

The scale is 14,200 / lens_px. Because the division is by the pixel value, the
low microns per pixel goes with the HIGH pixel diameter and the high with the
low. Every step of the Phase-1 error budget is carried through.

| Error source | Lens px end | Implied microns per pixel at 14,200 |
|---|---|---|
| Frame to frame spread, IQR | 764.4 to 809.5 | 17.54 to 18.58 |
| Method range on the same images | 760.0 to 789.0 | 18.00 to 18.68 |
| Population, library against production | 777.3 to 809.5 | 17.54 to 18.27 |
| Central, the median lens | 789.0 | 18.00 |

Combined interval: **17.54 to 18.68 microns per pixel, central reading 18.00**.

The nominal's own uncertainty is unquantified, because the only spread ever
attached to it is the uncited 14.0 to 14.3 mm range. If that range is taken at
face value it adds about 1.1 percent each side and widens the interval to 17.29
to 18.82. That widened figure is shown only so the reader can see how much an
uncited range would buy; it is not adopted.

### The gate-inversion scale

The same boundary read as a clean length: scale = cut_um / boundary_px, for the
new 300 micron cut and the older 647 micron cut.

| Source of the boundary | Boundary px | Implied microns per pixel | Central |
|---|---|---|---|
| New 300 cut, FM and Fiber intersected | 19.41 to 19.61 | 15.30 to 15.46 | 15.38 |
| Old 647 cut, FM and Fiber intersected | 42.08 to 42.79 | 15.12 to 15.38 | 15.25 |
| Combined | - | **15.30 to 15.38** | **15.34** |

This route agrees with neither 18.1165 nor 18.25. Its assumptions are the ones
ticket 014 already recorded: the gate acts on the largest base-code box, the box
edges delimit the debris, the declared cut is exact, and the fitting proxy is the
longer side. The boundary itself is sharp, with zero pictures on the wrong side,
so the interval is the boundary width plus the assumption risk, not sampling
noise.

### The gate read back into microns at each candidate scale

At a scale S, the 19.41 to 19.61 px switch corresponds to 19.41 x S to 19.61 x S
microns, and a 300 micron debris spans 300 / S px. The scale is named in the
first column of every row.

| Scale named | Scale value | Cut the switch reads as | A 300 micron debris spans |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lens measurement central | 18.00 | 349.4 to 353.0 microns | 16.67 px |
| Published value | 18.1165 | 351.6 to 355.3 microns | 16.56 px |
| Spoken value | 18.25 | 354.2 to 357.9 microns | 16.44 px |
| Gate inversion central | 15.34 | 297.7 to 300.8 microns | 19.56 px |

So at the lens-implied scale the client's gate boundary reads as about 349 to 353
microns, about 18 percent above the 300 the client declared. At the gate-inversion
scale it reads as 300, by construction.

### The tension, stated and not resolved

The lens measurement implies about **18.00 microns per pixel** at the assumed
14,200 micron nominal. The gate inversion implies about **15.34**. The gate value
sits about **14.8 percent below** the lens value, and the lens value sits about
**17.3 percent above** the gate value. The two are roughly 15 percent apart. This
is NOT resolved.

The two readings it allows:

- **Reading A.** The scale is the gate inversion, about 15.34 microns per pixel
  (interval 15.30 to 15.38). The 789 px lens is then about 12.1 mm, and the
  client gate is a clean 300 micron cut that reads correctly at that scale. The
  cost is a lens diameter well below the usual soft-lens range and a lens
  measurement that is about 15 percent too large.
- **Reading B.** The scale is the lens measurement, about 18.00 microns per pixel
  (interval 17.54 to 18.68) from our assumed 14.2 mm lens. The client gate
  boundary then reads as about 349 to 353 microns instead of 300, so the
  annotation boxes overshoot the 300 micron cut by about 14 to 15 percent. The
  cost is that the annotator's boxes are systematically larger than the debris,
  or our scale is about 15 percent too high.

The evidence that would separate them:

1. a written lens diameter from the client, for example the FMD Annotation Guide
   named in our records but not present, or any document naming the real lens size;
2. any frame showing two genuine cavities or the package or tray edge, giving a
   second ruler independent of the lens;
3. a lens product name or part number that fixes the diameter.

## Phase 4, the four falsification tests

### Test 1, the lens spec itself

If the scale were the gate inversion, the 789 px lens would be about 12.1 mm. So
the question is whether a 12.1 mm soft lens is plausible.

Searched: the two received documents (`docs/FM3 Edge Algorithm URS Final Draft
.pdf`, `docs/Manufacturing Condition Ontology V1.docx`) and this repo's records
(`docs/reference/received_docs_nominals.md`, `docs/reference/scale_provenance_20261015.md`).

What exists: the URS names the product only as a contact lens and describes the
material only through HEMA, the excess lens material removed at demold (section
5.2.1). The Ontology gives no lens dimension, only annotation-box size statistics
in pixels. The received set carries no lens diameter, no soft or rigid label and
no product identifier.

What does not exist: no received document names the lens product, its size, its
material beyond HEMA or its type.

**Result: not available from the received documents.** The physical plausibility
of 12.1 mm against 14.2 mm cannot be settled from our documents, because no
received document states a lens diameter. The general product class does not
favour 12.1 mm: a soft hydrogel contact lens is normally about 13.8 to 14.5 mm
across, so a 12.1 mm lens would sit below the usual range and 14.2 mm is the more
plausible diameter for a soft lens, while neither is documented here. The test
therefore leans toward reading B, on product-class grounds and not on evidence in
hand.

### Test 2, multiple lenses

The `P1_ML` class marks 1,439 pictures where more than one lens is present. Two
identical parts in one frame would give a within-frame repeatability of the lens
diameter and a check on whether the lens or the cavity is the stable reference.

A sample of 150 Multiple Lenses pictures and 150 single-lens Clear control
pictures was measured with `vm/ops/scan_multiple_lenses.py`, which runs the
Phase-1 rim circle fit and a two-circle split of the same rim edge points, plus a
full dark-ring inventory.

| Measure | Multiple Lenses | Clear control |
|---|---|---|
| Frames measured ok | 148 | 149 |
| Rim circle diameter, median | 791.1 px | 808.6 px |
| Rim circle diameter, IQR | 772.1 to 816.4 px | 789.1 to 820.2 px |
| Frames where a two-circle split improves the residual | 0.0 percent | 0.0 percent |
| Median split ratio (rms1 / rms2) | 0.81 | 0.74 |
| Frames with a dark ring in the cavity interior (norm below 0.80) | 38.5 percent | 11.4 percent |
| Interior rings found | 73 | 17 |
| Interior ring, median normalised radius | 0.58 | 0.74 |
| Within-frame rim residual, median | 19.6 px | 17.6 px |

**Result: the class does not present two resolvable full lens circles, so it does
not give the within-frame repeatability the test sought.** The split ratio is
below 1 in every frame of both groups, so splitting the rim edge into two circles
always fits worse, which means there is no second full rim to find. What the
class does contain, on 38.5 percent of frames against 11.4 percent of control, is
a single extra dark ring in the cavity interior at median 0.58 of the cavity
radius. That is consistent with the URS description of "one lens with an
additional partial lens" (section 5.1.12), but a partial lens at 0.58 R_cav is
not a second full lens and yields no diameter pair. With no two resolved rims
there is also no second measure of the same part to compare against the cavity,
so the lens-versus-cavity stability question is not answered by this class either.

### Test 3, a second ruler

A second ruler needs two fixed features in one frame whose real spacing is known.
A second cavity would show as a second bright component comparable in size to the
first; a package or tray edge would show as the largest bright component touching
the frame border. The 571 frame sample (120 library, 120 production drop, 150
Multiple Lenses, 150 Clear, 31 Package Find Test) was measured for exactly that.

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Frames measured ok | 271 |
| Frames with more than one bright component | 88 |
| Frames with a second component at least half the largest | 2 |
| Frames with the largest component touching the frame border | 0 |
| Median separation of the top two components | 545.1 px |

**Result: no second ruler available.** The two frames whose second component
reaches half the largest are Package Misalignment frames where the field of view
is partly blocked, not a second cavity. No frame has the largest bright component
touching the frame border, so no package or tray edge is imaged in frame. The
many small second components sit at a near-fixed position above the cavity and
are package features whose real length is unknown, so they give no scale either.
Every frame is one cavity, matching the `CavityNN` naming of the production drop.
Even if a second cavity were present, its real separation is written in no
received document, so it could not be turned into microns without a new nominal.

### Test 4, the box margin

At a scale S the gate switches on a longest box side of 19.41 to 19.61 px. A 300
micron debris at scale S spans 300 / S px. The gap between them is the annotation
margin the client's boxes would need.

| Scale named | Scale | A 300 micron debris spans | Required margin, px | Required margin, percent of box |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lens measurement central | 18.00 | 16.67 px | 2.74 to 2.94 px | 14.06 to 15.09 percent |
| Published value | 18.1165 | 16.56 px | 2.85 to 3.05 px | 14.61 to 15.64 percent |
| Spoken value | 18.25 | 16.44 px | 2.97 to 3.17 px | 15.23 to 16.26 percent |
| Gate inversion central | 15.34 | 19.56 px | -0.15 to 0.05 px | -0.77 to 0.25 percent |

**Result: the margin test does not separate the readings, and it makes the 18
micron scale the more comfortable one rather than the less.** The number that
decides it is 1.42 px per side. At 18.00 microns per pixel the client's box would
need to be about 2.74 to 2.94 px larger than the debris, that is about 1.42 px
overshoot on each side of a speck about 17 px across. A hand-drawn box
overshooting a small speck by about a pixel and a half per side is a normal,
tight margin, so a 14 to 15 percent systematic overshoot is plausible and does
not falsify the lens-implied scale. At 15.34 microns per pixel the required
margin is essentially zero, which is also plausible. The margin test is therefore
consistent with both readings and decides neither.

## Verification

1. **Every candidate is labelled and no assumed nominal is presented as
   documented.** `scale_candidates.json` carries 2 documented (the 300 and 647
   micron cuts), 1 assumed (the 14,200 micron lens) and 2 not found. The verifier
   asserts that every candidate whose class is documented has `is_documented`
   true and every assumed or not-found candidate has it false.
2. **Every scale figure is an interval with its error budget, and every micron
   figure names its scale.** The lens interval 17.54 to 18.68 carries the frame,
   method and population budget in one table; the gate interval 15.30 to 15.38
   carries the boundary and cut; the reverse-gate table and the box-margin table
   each name the scale in the first column. The verifier checks the naming
   structurally and re-derives each interval by its own arithmetic.
3. **The 15 percent tension is stated with the numbers on both sides and is NOT
   resolved.** Lens 18.00 against gate 15.34, gate 14.8 percent below lens, lens
   17.3 percent above gate; two readings named; three separating-evidence items.
   The verifier asserts the resolution field says NOT resolved.
4. **All four falsification tests end in a result or an explicit not available.**
   Test 1 not available from the received documents, with the reason. Test 2 a
   result with numbers. Test 3 no second ruler available, with numbers. Test 4 a
   result with the deciding number.
5. **The box-margin calculation gives the margin in pixels and as a percent at
   each candidate scale.** Four scales, both units, re-derived by the verifier.
6. **Byte-identical rebuild, md5 both times, and the verifier re-derives the
   headline intervals by a second route with a check and failure count.** Zero
   failures required.

the rebuild, md5 unchanged.

## Pitfalls, stated so they are not repeated

- **An assumption is not a nominal.** 14,200 microns is our figure. Nothing here
  may be read as a calibrated measurement; every micron figure is proportional,
  and if the true lens diameter differs, all of them move by the same factor.
- **A threshold is not a ruler.** The 300 and 647 micron cuts are documented, but
  they are pass/fail thresholds, not the size of a pictured object. They act as a
  ruler only through the annotation box boundary, which rests on the inversion
  assumptions in Phase 3.
- **The Multiple Lenses class is a partial-lens class, not a two-full-lens
  class.** The extra boundary is at 0.58 R_cav, well inside the lens rim.
- **No second ruler exists in this set.** Every frame is one cavity; no frame
  shows the package or tray edge.
